How to choose your processor

8 min read

The processor is the brain of your PC: it determines smoothness, stability, and much of how your build will feel over time. Choosing it well doesn't mean buying the most expensive one, but the one that best balances with the rest of your build. Here's what actually matters.

Cores and threads: how many do I need?

Cores are like "workers" inside the processor: each one can handle a task. Threads are each core's ability to handle more than one thing at a time. More cores help when you're doing several heavy things in parallel (editing video, streaming while gaming, etc.).

But here's a fact that surprises many people: for gaming, you don't need the most cores possible. Most games make good use of 6 to 8 cores, and beyond that the gaming benefit is small. A modern 6- or 8-core processor is more than enough for gaming. 12- or 16-core chips make sense if you also do heavy creative work, not just gaming.

Gaming's secret ingredient: cache

This is the factor most people overlook and that matters most for games. Cache is ultra-fast memory inside the processor. When the CPU needs game data, it looks there first; the more cache it has, the less it has to wait, which translates into smoother gameplay.

That's why, in 2026, AMD processors with stacked cache technology (the ones with "X3D" in the name) are gaming kings: that extra cache gives them a real performance edge in games, sometimes noticeably ahead of more expensive chips without that technology. If gaming is your top priority, a processor with lots of cache usually outperforms one with more cores but less cache.

Ad space

AMD or Intel: which is better?

Both brands are good and both use DDR5 RAM. The practical difference in 2026:

  • AMD clearly leads in pure gaming thanks to its stacked-cache (X3D) models. If gaming is your focus, it's usually the stronger bet.
  • Intel is very competitive in productivity tasks and offers solid mid-range options, with integration for AI tasks.

There's no single right answer: it depends on your budget and whether you do other heavy things besides gaming. Both will give you a great experience.

The socket: don't overlook it

Every processor uses a specific socket, and the motherboard must have that same socket. Current AMD chips use socket AM5; Intel Core Ultra chips use LGA1851. One point in AMD's favor: its AM5 platform has a longer upgrade path, meaning you're more likely to be able to upgrade to a newer processor in the future without replacing the motherboard. Always check that the CPU's socket and the motherboard's socket match — it's the golden rule of compatibility.

Balance with the graphics card

A common mistake is spending everything on the processor and skimping on the graphics card (or the other way around). For gaming, the GPU is usually the more decisive part, so the processor should keep up without stealing budget from the card. The idea is balance: a mid-range CPU with a good GPU performs better, for gaming, than a very expensive CPU with a weak GPU.

Don't forget cooling

More powerful processors generate more heat and need cooling to match. Some don't include a cooler in the box, so confirm whether yours does or whether you need to budget for one separately. An overheating processor throttles its performance to protect itself, so cooling it properly is part of choosing it well.

In summary

For gaming: prioritize cache over core count, 6-8 cores are enough, check the socket against your motherboard, balance it with the GPU, and factor in cooling. You don't need the top-of-the-line model for a great experience — you need the right processor for your use case.
