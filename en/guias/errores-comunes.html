Common mistakes when building your first PC

8 min read

Building your first PC is exciting, but it's also easy to trip over mistakes others have already made before you. The good news: almost all of them are avoidable once you know what to look for. Here are the most frequent ones, explained so you don't fall into them.

1. Skimping too much on the power supply

It's the classic mistake. After spending on the processor and the graphics card, many people want to cut costs on the power supply by buying the cheapest one they can find. It's a bad idea: the PSU powers every other component, and a low-quality one can be unstable or, in the worst case, damage the rest of the build. You don't need the most expensive one, but you do need one from a reliable brand, with certification (80+ Bronze at minimum) and enough watts for your build with margin to spare. Think of it as a house's foundation: you don't see it, but it holds everything up.

2. Forgetting cooling (or underestimating it)

Some processors don't include cooling in the box, and more than one person has been surprised while building their PC to discover that piece is missing. Others buy a cooler that's too weak for a powerful processor, which ends up overheating and losing performance to protect itself. Before buying, confirm whether your processor includes a cooler, and if not, budget for one that matches the heat it generates.

3. Not checking compatibility before buying

Buying a processor and a motherboard with a different socket, or RAM the board doesn't support, is one of the most expensive mistakes — because it means returning parts or being stuck with something you can't use. The rule is simple: before you pay, verify everything fits together. We have a full compatibility guide, and our configurator checks this for you automatically as you choose parts.

4. Unbalancing the budget

Spending almost everything on an extremely powerful graphics card and leaving scraps for everything else is a rookie mistake. An expensive GPU paired with a very weak processor creates a bottleneck: the card can't perform at its best because the processor can't keep up. The key is balance: every part should be roughly at the same level as the others. A balanced build beats one with a star part and everything else lagging.

5. Buying too little RAM (or too much)

Sticking with 8 GB of RAM today will leave you short in many current games. But going to the other extreme and buying 64 GB "just in case" when you're only going to game is throwing money at something that would perform better spent on the graphics card. For gaming, 16 GB is the comfortable minimum and 32 GB is the sweet spot with room for the future. Adjust based on what you'll actually do.

6. Ignoring case size

Falling in love with a huge graphics card and finding out it doesn't fit in your case is more common than you'd think. High-end GPUs are long, and premium air coolers are tall. Before buying, check that the case has enough room for the card's length and the cooler's height. A detail that gets overlooked and ruins the build.

7. Obsessing over "today's" prices

Component prices change constantly, especially in today's market. Rushing to buy a "deal" without comparing, or endlessly waiting for the perfect price that never comes, are two sides of the same mistake. Set your budget, compare across two or three stores, and buy when you find a reasonable price. The perfect PC at the perfect price doesn't exist; a good PC at a fair price does.

8. Not planning before buying

The mistake that includes all the others: buying individual parts without a well-thought-out list. Put together your full build on paper (or in a configurator) before spending a single dollar. That way you see the real total, catch incompatibilities, and avoid surprises. Five minutes of planning saves you weeks of headaches.
